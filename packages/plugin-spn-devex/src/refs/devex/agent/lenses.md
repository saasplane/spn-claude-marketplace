# Lenses — worn while writing, convened at review

**Source of truth:** the foundation's `02-constructs/01-devex/02-agent/03-lenses.md` and `04-capabilities/01-devex/02-agent/03-lenses/01-lenses.md`. Read this as the restatement; the book governs.

**The eleven lens files sit beside this one, in `refs/lenses/`.** This says what a lens *is* and what convening one may do — the files themselves say what each one checks.

## One file per engineering function

**The set is generated by a rule, never by a count.** One lens per engineering function that builds or runs a platform. So no sentence anywhere says how many there are: a function added is a lens added, and a count written down is a count that stops being true.

**The DevEx Agent is not an actor.** It works *with* each of them; it is not one of them.

**Actors build; personas use.** A lens is an actor's view. A persona is somebody the platform serves, and personas belong to the behaviour rows rather than to the review.

## Wearing and convening are two different acts

**Wearing a lens while writing makes the work better, and is never a review** — because the context that drafted something already agrees with its own reasoning. A lens that is only ever worn is never a review.

**Convening opens a fresh reviewer**, and that is the reason to convene one: it reads the work without having written it.

**A panel reports; the writing context acts.** The panel does not edit, and it does not decide what happens next.

**A panel never invents a rule.** A finding with no owning chapter behind it is a suggestion, and it is reported as one.

## What a lens may block

**Almost nothing.** Most lenses advise, and their findings arrive as decision cards rather than as refusals.

**The voice is checked twice and only one of them blocks** — the write-time check, which is mechanical. The voice lens is judgement, and judgement advises.

## Why the files ship here

**A partner holds the plugins and not the book.** A lens file is the reviewer's whole brief, so it ships whole — and it is filed by **role** rather than by construct, which is why `lenses/` reports no coverage number. There is no construct for it to be named after.
